A 12-week implementation roadmap

The sequence most HR teams follow when transitioning to the new codes.

WK 0–2

Regulatory readiness

Stand up a cross-functional taskforce (HR, ER, Legal, Payroll, EHS). Map which establishments and states are affected, and track central/state notifications as they land.

WK 3–6

Contracts, payroll & policy

Restructure CTC so basic+DA reaches the 50% wage threshold; model the PF/ESI/gratuity/bonus cost impact; issue appointment letters; rewrite leave, OT and grievance policies; refresh fixed-term templates.

WK 7–10

OSH & vendor governance

Roll out hazard-specific medical check-ups, activate safety committees where thresholds apply, and launch a contractor compliance protocol (PF/ESI verification, wage audits, amenities).

WK 11–12

Change management

Train HR, ER and line managers on new entitlements and documentation discipline; communicate rights and grievance routes to the workforce; prepare for inspector-cum-facilitator audits.

Status note: Provisions of each code come into force on dates the Central and State Governments notify — not automatically from the year each code was enacted. Treat every threshold and figure here as the statutory design; always confirm current notification status for your state and sector before acting. Type a Gross/CTC figure and the Basic, DA, HRA, Conveyance, Special and Overtime fields below fill themselves automatically with a compliant split (Basic+DA at least 50% of Gross, per Section 2(y) of the Code on Wages). Edit any field afterwards to fine-tune.
Based on the uniform "wages" definition in Section 2(y) of the Code on Wages (mirrored in the Code on Social Security): components such as HRA, conveyance, special allowances, overtime and commission are excluded from "wages" only up to 50% of total remuneration. Anything above that cap is added back for computing PF, gratuity, bonus and overtime. This calculator illustrates the mechanism — it does not replace a payroll audit.

Employee vs. "worker" classifier

Many IR Code protections (Standing Orders, grievance redressal, retrenchment compensation) apply only to "workers" — a narrower category than "employee".
